## Competitors (what people use today for the same task)

### 1. Calling and messaging people one by one · no app
- Link: none. Screenshot: a blurred chat (no names, no faces) or a photo of your call list
- What I take from it: it is what my group really does today, and it works because everyone gets a personal answer
- What I would avoid: the same question asked 10 to 20 times, and the answers living in separate chats

### 2. WhatsApp group poll · WhatsApp
- Link: in the app, Attach > Poll. Source: https://chatfuel.com/blog/create-whatsapp-polls
- What I take from it: everyone votes with one tap, results update live, and you can see who voted for what
- What I would avoid: a poll holds one question with up to 12 options, so "which event" and "will you drink" need two separate polls, and the poll gets lost in chat history

### 3. Partiful · iOS app
- Link: https://apps.apple.com/app/id1662982304
- What I take from it: after you RSVP you see who else is going, and guests can join through a link without installing the app
- What I would avoid: it is built for one host and one event, not for a group choosing between several events on the same night; its payment links point to Venmo, PayPal and Cash App (check whether that fits Hungary)

### 4. RA Guide · Resident Advisor, iOS app
- Link: https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/ra-guide/id981952703
- What I take from it: an event list made for electronic music, with an "Interested" button on every event
- What I would avoid: the interest is personal, so it does not show what my friends chose; it also pushes ticket buying, which is out of scope for me

## Patterns (one screen that solves one part of my task)

### 5. "Going" counter on an event list · Techno Airlines, Budapest events
- Link: https://www.technoairlines.com/events/budapest
- The pattern: each event row shows its time, its venue and how many people marked themselves as going
- Why it fits my app: my list of daily events can show how many of my friends chose each one, so the group sees the winner without asking

### 6. Yes / no / maybe voting · Doodle or Rallly
- Link: https://doodle.com and https://rallly.co
- The pattern: each person gives one of a few answers to every option, and the totals show where the group overlaps
- Why it fits my app: the answer to "do you want to go" can be yes, no or maybe, and the drinking answer can use the same quick tap

### 7. Group balance and equal split · Splitwise
- Link: https://splitwise.com
- The pattern: a group, an amount, and an equal split between the people who take part
- Why it fits my app: if I know how many people will drink, the shared purchase can be split by that number. Note: Splitwise users complain that everyone has to register, which is a warning for a group of 10 to 20
